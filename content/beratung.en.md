Persistent pain can be unsettling – especially when medical examinations do not provide a sufficient explanation. In one-to-one counselling we look at your pain together, with a perspective that considers body, nervous system and emotions as a whole.

## What is Pain Reprocessing Therapy?

Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT) was developed by Alan Gordon at the Pain Psychology Center in Los Angeles. The physician Dr Howard Schubiner has worked with related approaches for many years and was involved in research on PRT.

The approach assumes that in some chronic pain conditions the brain and nervous system play a major role. Pain is a protective signal. Sometimes this signal persists even though there is no (longer any) tissue damage. Fear, tension and stress can amplify it – and the pain in turn amplifies the fear.

**The pain is always real.** It is not about "thinking it away", but about inviting the nervous system to new, safe experiences.

## What we do in the sessions

- **Understanding:** We talk about how pain arises and what role the nervous system plays.
- **Gathering evidence:** Together we look at what in your everyday life fits this understanding – and what does not.
- **Somatic tracking:** You practise noticing bodily sensations mindfully and with curiosity, with a sense of safety rather than alarm.
- **Including stress:** Pressure and difficult emotions can also play a part. We look at them gently.
- **Strengthening safety:** Pleasant sensations, self-compassion and moments of calm are given conscious space.

You set the pace. Breaks and limits are always welcome.

## Who is it for?

The counselling is for adults with persistent pain that has already been **assessed by a doctor**. PRT is not suitable for every kind of pain. In an introductory conversation we find out together whether the approach suits your situation.
